# 5.1 Help Me Carry

Source: RoboCup@Home Rulebook 2025.1, Final, Rev 2025-04-28, section 5.1, pp. 39-41.
Score sheet: RoboCup@Home Forms & Score Sheets, Final, Rev 2025-04-28, Help Me Carry page (PDF p. 5).
The rulebook is the source of truth. This page restates it and says what the simulation covers.

## Goal

The robot helps the operator carry a bag from a car parked outside to the starting location.

- Main goal: bring a bag from the car.
- Optional goals: (1) re-reach the starting location, (2) follow the queue on the way back.
- Focus: person following, navigation in unmapped environments, social navigation.

## Setup

- Locations: the test is outside the arena in a public space. The robot starts at a predefined location.
- People: the operator stands in front of the robot.
- Objects: at least two bags near the car, a small object on the ground, a hard-to-see object, retractable barriers.
- Uncontrolled environment: other people may walk by or stand around during the whole task.

## Procedure

1. Following the operator. The robot informs the operator when it is ready to follow. The operator walks naturally to the car, then points at the bag.
2. Obstacles. The robot meets 4 obstacles in arbitrary order: (a) small object on the ground, (b) hard-to-see object, (c) a crowd blocking the path, (d) a small area blocked with retractable barriers.
3. Picking up the bag. The robot picks up the bag the operator points at.
4. Bag delivery. The robot takes the bag to the starting location. It may place the bag on the floor.
5. Optional goal, following the queue. After the robot reaches the car, a few people from the crowd return to the start in a queue. The robot may join the queue in a way that looks natural to them.

## Additional rules

1. Car location. There is no real car. A fixed location outside the arena is the car location.
2. Reaching the car. The robot can reach the car only by following the operator. The location is unknown before the test.
3. Deus ex Machina. Score is reduced when the robot loses the operator and finds them again through:
   1. natural interaction (waving, calling), -50
   2. unnatural interaction (raising both hands, jumping), -100
   3. coming back (asking the operator to step back in front of the robot), -150
   4. touching the robot (pulling its hand), -200

   Penalties apply only if the robot keeps scoring after the interaction. They can repeat, up to a total of 250 points.

## Time limit

The maximum time for this test is **5:00 minutes**.

## Tries and restart

The score sheet has columns for a 1st, 2nd and 3rd try, and records the duration and the "restart after (sec)" time of each try.

The restart rule is in rulebook sec. 3.7.9, pp. 25-26. During a test, a team may do one restart per attempt, under these conditions:

- A restart may only be requested until the robot completes a major scoring action.
- The test time keeps running until the team has left the arena with the robot.
- A restart costs 30 s, subtracted from the total time.
- After leaving, the team may queue again at the back of the line and start the attempt again in its remaining time.
- Only one restart is allowed per attempt.
- Only points scored after the restart count. Points scored before the restart are dropped.

The simulation runs one try with no restart. The score sheet code models neither tries nor restarts.

## How the simulation interprets the rulebook

These are choices of this project, not rulebook text.

- "Reached the car" is judged by the referee code: the robot is within 3 m of the car point. The robot code never reads the car point.
- "Perceived the correct bag" is awarded when the bag the task perceived matches the bag the operator points at. The task says which bag it sees in the same step. The referee does not check the speech log.
- "Re-reached the start" needs the robot within 1 m of the start, after the car was reached, with the target bag within 1 m of the start too.
- Putting the bag down at the start on purpose is not a drop. A drop happens only through `manipulation.slip()`, which tests use.
- The scripted world has the four obstacles of the procedure on the way to the car. The operator steps over a small object (circle, r 0.12 m), walks closely around a hard-to-see object (circle, r 0.15 m), passes through a crowd of three people whose gaps fit a person but not the robot, and walks around a box of retractable barriers.
- The robot sees obstacles only as shapes, through perception. It never learns their kind. The hard-to-see object is only seen within 1.5 m of its edge. The robot plans around everything it sees (A* on a 0.2 m grid, inflated by the robot radius 0.26 m plus 0.15 m).
- An "avoid" row is judged when the robot reaches the car. It is awarded when the robot came within 1.0 m of that obstacle (it faced it) and never touched it. Touching means the obstacle edge was closer than the robot radius (0.26 m). For the crowd, the edges of the people count.
- The queue: 2 s after the robot reaches the car, the three people of the crowd start walking back, 2 s apart, at 0.5 m/s. Their route goes past the start and on, so the queue does not block the start.
- The robot joins the queue when it sees walking people ahead of it (closer to the start than itself). It follows the closest of them at 1.0 m. It leaves the queue 2 m from the start and then goes to the start.
- The "queue" row is awarded when the robot got within 2.0 m behind the last person of the queue, then stayed behind them, never more than 3.0 m away and never touching anyone, until it was 2.0 m from the start.
- There are six maps (`--map`). `default` is a small made-up scenario. The others copy the official Carry My Luggage maps from github.com/RoboCupAtHome: `bangkok2022`, `bordeaux2023`, `eindhoven2024`, `germanopen2024` and `portugalopen2024`. Their walls and furniture are obstacles of kind `wall`, which have no score row. The official maps never show the car or the outside, so the scale (only German Open 2024 gives one), the outside part, the car and the obstacles are our own choice. Outside the exit, the maps reuse the default course, turned away from the door. German Open 2025 is left out: its official map is a placeholder.
- In Bangkok 2022, German Open 2024 and Portugal Open 2024 the robot starts inside the flat and must first leave through the exit door. Doors are 1.0 m wide; the rulebook (sec. 3.3.2) recommends 0.915 m. The queue comes back in through the same door and ends next to the start.
- Walls block the view of the operator, the people and the bags. If the robot loses sight of the last person of the queue for up to 3 s (a corner), it keeps going to where it last saw them. In the queue it stands 1.0 m behind the last person, in the direction that person walks.
- The queue part of the way back ends, and the "queue" row is judged, when the robot or the last person is within 2.0 m of the start, or when the last person has reached the end of their route.
- Passers-by (`--bystanders N`). The setup says "There are no restrictions on other people walking by or standing around throughout the complete task." Passers-by walk between random free spots outside, wait sometimes, and step away from the robot when it comes within 1.2 m. They give no points.
- How the robot tells the queue from passers-by. The rulebook says "The queue is composed of the same people that form the crowd." So on the way out the robot remembers the people who stood still for at least 6 s in a group (within 1.5 m of each other) within 4 m of it. On the way back only walking people who look like one of them count as the queue.
- Sensor noise (`--noise realistic|hard`): position error, missed detections (more often beyond 6 m), pointing error, tracker id swaps between people who pass within 0.8 m, and frames where someone close to the operator is taken for the operator. The levels are our guess for a small YOLO detector with a simple tracker, not measurements.
- Re-identification. Every person has a random look (a 16-number unit vector, like a re-ID embedding). Perception reports it with noise, and the look stays with the real person when tracker ids are swapped. The robot learns the operator's look in the first 20 frames and ignores operator detections that look different. It remembers the crowd by look, not by tracker id.
- Handling noise in the robot: positions are averaged over 0.5 s, operator detections that jump further than a person can walk are ignored, the pointed bag needs 5 votes and a lead of 3, and a person missed for up to 1 s is still avoided.
- "Faced" for the avoid rows means the robot came within 1.5 m of the obstacle on the way out. The way out ends when the target bag is picked, because going around people the robot may get near the car before it passes the last obstacle.
- The "queue" row needs the robot to join with the bag, within 2.0 m behind the last person, and to stay behind them, never more than 3.0 m away and never touching anyone, until the queue part ends.
- The real robot's sensors (`--sensors robot`, see `src/hmc/sensors.py`): an RGB camera with a detector, 58 degrees forward, people and bags up to 6 m, the pointing arm up to 4 m, and the only source of looks for re-ID; the Xtion depth image, 58 degrees forward, 0.6 m to 3.5 m, the only sensor that sees the small object, the hard-to-see object and the belt of the barriers; a 2D lidar, 360 degrees, 8 m, in a plane 0.22 m above the floor, which sees walls, legs and the barrier posts. The robot remembers obstacles it saw for 10 s, faces the operator while it waits behind them, turns to read the arm, and then looks along the arm to find the bag.
- Odometry (`--odometry perfect|wheel|slam`, see `src/hmc/odometry.py`): the robot only knows where it believes it is. `wheel` integrates the motion with a 2 % distance error, a 1 % turn error and a slow heading drift. `slam` adds a correction every second that leaves about 5 cm of error, a simple stand-in for a real SLAM system. Perception reports everything in the believed frame, and the start is remembered in that frame, so a drifted robot can miss the start. The referee always uses the truth.
- Robot speed. `--robot-speed` sets the top speed (default 1.0 m/s; the real base is capped at 0.15 m/s and 15 deg/s today). The base cannot change speed at once: it speeds up and brakes at up to 0.5 m/s^2, and its turn rate (up to 2 rad/s) changes by up to 3 rad/s^2, which is the same wheel acceleration on a two-wheel base about 0.34 m wide. It still turns on the spot. While rolling it brakes in time for the goal and for anything closer than 0.02 m to its edge straight ahead, both where it faces and where it is turning to. When the path turns back behind it (more than 90 degrees), it first brakes to a stop, rolling straight on, and then turns. It aims at a waypoint up to 0.4 m ahead on the path, so it does not wobble.
- People's speed. The operator walks 1.2 m/s by nature (`--operator-speed`), passers-by 1.0 to 1.4 m/s. The queue still walks 0.5 m/s.
- The robot manages the operator's pace. The rulebook says the referees instruct the operator to "follow the robots instructions". Right after "I am ready to follow you." the robot says "Please walk slowly, at about X metres per second, so I can follow you.", with X = 0.8 x its top speed. The operator then walks at X, or at their own speed if that is slower.
- While following, when the robot sees it is more than 4 m behind the operator and it has a path, it says "Please wait for me." and the operator stands still. Once the gap is below 2.5 m, or when the robot has no path (the operator may be standing in the door it needs), it says "Thank you, please go on." and the operator walks on.
- A person of the queue waits rather than walk into the robot. The Deus ex Machina cap (-250) is implemented in the score sheet. The "only if the robot keeps scoring" condition is not modelled, because the simulation never triggers a Deus ex Machina event.
- A person of the queue waits rather than walk into the robot. The Deus ex Machina cap (-250) is implemented in the score sheet.
- Losing the operator. While following, if the robot has not seen the operator for more than 2 s, it goes to state `search`. It drives to where it saw them last (for at most 10 s, the spot may be blocked), then turns on the spot for 5 s, because the robot camera sees only 58 degrees forward. If it still has not found them, it says "I lost you. Please come back and stand in front of me." once and waits facing forward. It takes a detection as the operator again only when it comes with a look that matches the operator's (re-ID), so a stranger is not followed. Then it goes back to `follow`.
- The scripted operator reacts to that sentence: they walk in a straight line to a spot 1.5 m in front of the robot, wait there 2 s, walk back to where they were and go on with their route. The straight line may cross a wall; a real volunteer would walk around it.
- Only Deus ex Machina 3 ("coming back", -150) is simulated, because it is the only one the robot causes by talking. The referee counts it when the operator has come back because the robot asked, and awards it only when a main or bonus row is awarded after that (the rulebook note "only applied if the robot continues to score"). Asking more often is allowed; the -250 cap still holds. Natural interaction, unnatural interaction and direct contact stay `not-run`.
